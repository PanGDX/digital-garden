Well, the first step to any job application is to **write or update your resume**. A resume is a **1- to 2-page long** document that tells the employer a summary of your professional experiences and qualifications.
Both a **_record of your past achievements_** and **_a marketing tool that job applicants use to_** **_communicate your unique value_** to prospective employers.


Recruiters glance through your resume. In 6 seconds. 

- Analyze the job description
- List down experience and skills with NUMBERS. PEOPLE LOVE NUMBERS.
- **Craft results-oriented Competency.Action.Result. statements**
- **Proof-read**


- No objectives. Don't talk about what you want, talk about what they need. 
- What are you great at and passionate about and why are you qualified.
- Use bullet points as bragging points.
- Entry level = 1 page
- Header should be larger (13-16)
- Body is around 10-12 font
- Half an inch borders



A lot of skills are transferable. Design skills. Communications skills. Data literacy skills. Data presentation skills. When reading a job posting, the first layer to peel back is what they need. What did they post and what does it indicate about what they need from me?
The second layer is about US. Read reviews regarding the company. If possible, try to hit people up in Linkedin from those companies. Recommendations are worth its weight in gold.



### Difference between CV and Resume
A CV:
- Has no length limit
- Is typically used for academic purposes
- Is the entire course of your career
A resume
- Is one or two pages long
- Only includes the highlights of your work and how it is relevant to the job
- Resumes are used to apply for jobs

### CAR Statements
**Competency**
Begin each bullet point with a **strong action verb** that demonstrates a competency or an attribute.
**Action**
**Summarise** **_how_** **you carried out the tasks** or duties, explain who you worked with and the tools used to achieve a positive outcome or a desired goal. You can include facts or details that show the **scope** of the project too, e.g. 'over a period of 3 weeks', or 'impacting 30 youth'.
**Results**
Whenever possible, **quantify the results** of your efforts by stating the contributions or achievements. For instance the amount that was raised, the hours spent, the months or days that the project spanned, the percentage of increase. NUMBERS ARE GREAT

- **Conducted English and Math lessons for five Primary 6 students and improved their grades from Band 3 to Band 1**

**Avoid**:
- Listing job description
- Being self-focused (what I learnt)
- Vagueness (no numbers)

### Interviewing
**8 types of interview formats**
- Traditional interview
	- Sitting across the interviewer. Asked normal interview questions. 
- Zoom interview
- Phone interview
- Group interview
	- Stand out. Speak up regularly and confidently
- Working interview
	- Do specific parts of the job before starting
- Case or puzzle interview
	- Solving programming questions, used to get a sense of the way you think
- Firing Squad
	- Meet with many interviewers in rapid succession. 
- Lunch interview



Collects info on facial expressions, eye & body movements, clothes, nuances of voice to predict the future job performance of the candidate

**Things to look out for!**

- Prepare as you would for a face-to-face interview.
- Check the technical connection.
- Pick the appropriate attire.
- Maintain eye contact with the camera lens.
- Enunciate words clearly and avoid stutter or long pauses.
- Watch your facial expressions & body language
- Rehearse & Practice – rapport & watch yourself

**Rules**
- Be punctual to give a good first impression
- Maintain eye contact
- Sharp and professional suit
- Small talk to build rapport
- Give a firm handshake
- Arrange them neatly in a file or a dossier so that they can be easily retrieved when asked for.

- Potential - how much can you grow

- Commitment, interest and loyalty

### Connections
- **88% of employers hired candidates through referrals**.
- Specifically relating to job search, **54%** received a job introduction through a casual conversation on LinkedIn and **52%** said they secured a job because they knew someone at the company.
- Job portals account for only 20-40% of all jobs available.  This means that **60-80% of jobs are not even posted**


**The best way to build a big network is to get involved with as many things that matter to you.**
**If you like to code, contribute on GitHub or take part in a hackathon.**

In particular, what Granovetter showed was that people with weak ties not only find jobs that the rest of the tight network cannot see, but that these jobs come with higher compensation and satisfaction.
